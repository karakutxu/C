This assessment compared passive scanner measurements with active User Equipment (UE) measurements collected during the Scottish Islands airborne trial to better understand the impact of the artificial 12 dB test loss, the relative performance of Bands 3, 20 and 40, and the expected operational behaviour under normal deployment conditions. Passive measurements provide an assessment of the underlying RF environment independent of UE serving-cell selection and mobility behaviour, while the active measurements represent operational network performance.
The comparison demonstrates that the artificial test configuration produced the expected reduction in received signal strength, with approximately 10 dB lower median RSRP observed during active testing. However, the corresponding degradation in RSRQ and SINR was considerably smaller, indicating that signal quality is governed predominantly by propagation conditions, interference and network geometry rather than received power alone.
The passive measurements confirm that Band 20 generally provides substantially stronger signal strength than Band 3 across the surveyed area, although both bands exhibit broadly comparable signal quality. The difference between the passive and active results indicates that active measurements are influenced by the network's band-selection and mobility strategy, resulting in Band 3 being selected as the serving layer for the majority of the active measurements despite the stronger Band 20 RSRP.
Band 40 demonstrates good signal quality across much of the route and has the potential to provide an increasingly important airborne coverage layer as reliance on the terrestrial network reduces with altitude. However, sustained coverage nulls remain in several geographic regions, principally associated with sites that were either not yet brought into service or were not fully operational during testing. These areas are expected to limit continuous airborne coverage until the A2G network deployment is completed.
Overall, the passive measurements support the conclusions drawn from the active campaign and indicate that the active results represent a conservative assessment of airborne network performance.

1. Overall Site Coverage Assessment
Overall, the measurements indicate that the combined terrestrial and A2G network provides a good level of airborne RF coverage across much of the surveyed area. The terrestrial network, particularly Band 3, remained the dominant serving layer during the active campaign, while the passive measurements demonstrate that Band 20 offers stronger received signal strength but is selected less frequently during operation. Band 40 provides good RF performance over much of the route but continues to exhibit coverage gaps associated with incomplete A2G deployment and inter-site spacing.
________________________________________
1.1 RF Viewpoint
Comparison of the passive and active measurements demonstrates that the artificial 12 dB attenuation applied during active testing primarily affected received signal strength, with median RSRP improving by approximately 10 dB once the artificial loss was removed. In contrast, only relatively small changes were observed in RSRQ and SINR, confirming that signal quality is primarily determined by propagation geometry and interference rather than received power alone.
Passive measurements show that Band 20 consistently provides the strongest received signal levels, typically around 20 dB stronger than Bands 3 and 40. However, Band 20 signal quality is only comparable to, or marginally better than, Band 3, indicating that stronger signal strength alone does not necessarily translate into superior radio performance.
Band 40 exhibits lower median RSRP than Band 20 but generally higher median SINR than either terrestrial band, demonstrating good RF quality where A2G coverage is available. Nevertheless, sustained coverage nulls remain in several geographic regions corresponding to sites that were unavailable or only partially operational during the trial, reducing overall coverage continuity.
________________________________________
1.2 Operational Viewpoint
The passive measurements provide context for interpreting the active trial results by separating underlying RF conditions from UE mobility and serving-cell selection behaviour.
The predominance of Band 3 during the active campaign appears consistent with the network's mobility strategy, which favours Band 3 when both terrestrial bands provide acceptable coverage. Consequently, active comparisons between Bands 3 and 20 alone should be interpreted with caution, as the sampling distribution is inherently biased towards Band 3.
The passive measurements suggest that removal of the artificial test loss would improve the operational performance of Bands 20 and 40, both by increasing received signal strength and by improving opportunities for mobility to these layers. While Band 40 is expected to assume a more significant operational role at higher altitudes as terrestrial performance reduces, the remaining A2G coverage gaps indicate that service continuity may continue to be limited in specific areas until network deployment is completed.

2. Conclusions
•	The measured difference between passive and active RSRP closely matches the expected artificial test loss, confirming that the test configuration primarily affected received signal strength. 
•	Changes in RSRQ and SINR between passive and active measurements are comparatively small, indicating that signal quality is driven principally by interference, propagation geometry and serving-cell dominance rather than received power alone. 
•	Passive measurements confirm that Band 20 generally provides substantially stronger received signal strength than Band 3 across the surveyed area. However, comparable signal quality and the network's serving-cell selection strategy result in Band 3 remaining the dominant serving layer during active operation. 
•	Active measurements alone do not provide a statistically representative comparison between Bands 3 and 20 because the serving-cell selection process results in significantly more measurements being collected on Band 3. 
•	Band 40 demonstrates good airborne RF potential, particularly in terms of signal quality, but its coverage continuity is currently limited by sustained coverage gaps associated with incomplete A2G deployment and partially operational sites. 
•	The passive measurements indicate that the active campaign represents a conservative assessment of operational performance, as the artificial attenuation and serving-cell selection behaviour both reduce the observed performance of Bands 20 and 40.

3. Recommendations
•	Review airborne band-selection strategy. Assess whether the current mobility and band-selection parameters between Bands 3 and 20 remain optimal for airborne operation, considering both RF performance and operational service metrics. 
•	Complete A2G network deployment. Prioritise commissioning of outstanding Band 40 sites and resolution of partially operational sites, particularly those contributing to sustained coverage nulls (e.g. Dailburgh, Skriag and Kilchiaran). 
•	Reassess Band 40 performance following network completion. Repeat the analysis once the outstanding A2G sites are fully operational to quantify improvements in coverage continuity and identify any remaining airborne coverage gaps. 
•	Investigate higher-altitude performance. Conduct additional flight trials at representative operational altitudes to assess how the balance between terrestrial and A2G coverage changes as terrestrial contribution reduces, and to confirm whether Band 40 can provide sufficient standalone airborne continuity. 
•	Interpret active and passive measurements together. Future assessments should continue to use passive scanner measurements alongside active UE testing to distinguish underlying RF conditions from operational behaviour, mobility and serving-cell selection effects, providing a more complete understanding of network performance.
